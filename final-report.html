The short version

What happened with 4.2

On 12 August, 4.2 made a ping wait 60 seconds instead of 90. A missed ping then counted the same as a turn-down, and nothing ever gave the points back. So some responders slid out of the order and stayed there.

Why they never came back

Same responders, two scoring rules

My reconstruction from the code, not stored scores. Endpoints are the replay results; the path between is drawn straight.

Misses scored as declines The four fall from ~0.9 to 0.00 by early September.

Misses neutral They stay between 0.76 and 0.84.

Break-even is a 60% take rate. A miss costs −0.12; a take only earns +0.08. A Wen Li 2019 TODO in history.py flagged this years ago.

Still untested: whether the four are slow answerers or hit push failure. That's why the fix is staged.

Module 4 · X-Ray Vision

The code takes points off, and never puts them back

#23 Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
One penalty, one credit, no recovery.
  • record_declined (offer.py:30, history.py:39) takes −0.12, and it's called for anything not taken, misses included.
  • The only credit is record_accepted: +0.08.
  • No decay, floor of 0, and a score only moves when someone is offered a ping.
  • 4.2 cut the recent-acceptance weight 0.40 → 0.25 and raised proximity 0.45 → 0.60: roughly half the lock-out power, not zero.
#26 Somebody has been quiet for a month. Walk me through, step by step, exactly what would have to happen for them to start getting work again.
There is no way back in the code.
  • Nothing lets a score recover with time.
  • Only a taken ping adds credit (+0.08), and only if they are offered one.
  • So 'wait until September' has no mechanism behind it.

This also answers Marcus's 14 Aug question: the change applied to everyone, no tenure check.
